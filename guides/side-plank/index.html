Side Plank: 8 Variations, How Long to Hold It, Left vs Right

Updated November 24, 2026 · 12 minute read · Free PDF

A side plank is a plank on your side: one forearm and the edge of one foot on the floor, your body in a straight line from head to heels. It works the muscles along the side of your waist and hip, and in muscle studies it ranked among the best exercises for both. You do it twice, once on each side. In the studies below, fit young adults held it for about 1 to 2 minutes a side, and in one of them each person's two sides were 10 seconds apart on average. Here are 8 versions from easiest to hardest, how to do one well, how long people really hold it, what it does and does not do, and a 4 week plan.

Start with the first one you can hold for 20 seconds on each side with your body straight, and move down the list when that gets easy.

Rolling in and out of a side plank asked the most of three of the four trunk muscles measured in a 2014 study of 25 adults, the external oblique at 63% of its maximum (Youdas and colleagues, 2014). Raising the top leg made the side plank the best of 22 exercises for the gluteus medius, the muscle on the side of your hip: 103% of its maximum in the hip on the floor, 89% in the hip of the lifted leg (Boren and colleagues, 2011). How to do a side plank

This is the side plank as the studies below test it, on the forearm with the top foot in front:

  1. Lie on your side. Legs straight, the top foot just in front of the bottom one.
  2. Elbow under your shoulder. Forearm flat on the floor, pointing forward, elbow bent at a right angle.
  3. Lift your hips. Raise them until your body is one straight line from head to feet. Only your forearm and the edges of your feet touch the floor.
  4. Free hand. On your hip, or across your chest to the other shoulder.
  5. Hold and breathe. Keep your hips up and your body straight: no sagging toward the floor, no pushing your bottom back.
  6. Switch sides. Lower, rest, then hold the other side for the same time.

A hold is over when your hips drop or the straight line breaks. One study stopped the clock as soon as the pelvis moved 5 cm (2 inches) up or down (Luedke and Rauh, 2022). If your elbow hurts on a hard floor, use a mat. If your shoulder does, go back to the knee version.

How long should you hold a side plank?

Studies test the side plank by holding it until the hips drop. This is what young, active people managed:

140 US college athletes, 200484 s59 s

24 active women in Spain, 2022Not tested77 s, then 69 s a week later
45 recreational athletes in Spain, 2025128 s117 s

The college athletes were basketball players and track athletes (Leetun and colleagues, 2004), and the runners and jumpers were track and cross country athletes (Luedke and Rauh, 2022). The Spanish groups were tested by the same lab, the second after two practice tests (Juan-Recio and colleagues, 2022 and 2025). So for a fit young adult, a minute to two minutes a side is normal when tested to the limit. Three things move the number:

  • Practice. The 2025 group held about 5% longer on their second test than on their first, which the authors put down to learning the test.
  • Body size. Heavier men held for less time: in the 2025 study, body weight alone explained half the spread in the men's times.
  • The rules. The 2022 college study stopped every test at 120 seconds, and about a fifth to a quarter of its athletes were still holding.

Every study we found tested young, active adults: we found no side plank times by age for adults. For training, no study has set the best hold time either, and the American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 review found too little data to say how much endurance work is best (Currier and colleagues, 2026). The plan below holds for half your test time.

Left side vs right side

A side plank is two exercises. The side facing the floor does most of the work: in the 2014 study, nearly every muscle measured worked harder on the bottom side than on the top (Youdas and colleagues, 2014). So a left side plank, on your left forearm, trains your left side most.

Your two sides will rarely match. In the 75 college runners and jumpers, the right and left side planks averaged 75 seconds each, but each athlete's two sides were 10 seconds apart on average (Luedke and Rauh, 2022). The test also wobbles on its own: the same women, tested a week apart, varied by about 11 seconds from one test to the next, the study's typical error (Juan-Recio and colleagues, 2022).

So a gap of 10 seconds or so between your sides tells you little. A bigger gap that shows up test after test is worth watching. We found no study that tested extra work for the weaker side in healthy people, so train both sides for the same time, as RankTime does, and test again after 2 weeks.

Side plank muscles worked

Obliques and the ab wallThe side of your waist. The side plank was the best exercise for the whole ab wall in a 1998 study of deep muscles, while asking little of the psoas, the deep hip flexor attached to your lower spine (Juker and colleagues, 1998). In 24 women, the ab wall worked at 41 to 50% of its maximum through the hold (Juan-Recio and colleagues, 2022)
Gluteus mediusThe side of your hip. Over 69% of its maximum in three of four side bridge exercises (Youdas and colleagues, 2014), and 89 to 103% with the top leg lifting (Boren and colleagues, 2011)
Quadratus lumborumDeep in the lower back, between the ribs and the pelvis. The 2004 study of college athletes used the side plank as its endurance test
ShoulderThe bottom shoulder works hard too. In the 24 women, the deltoid tired as fast as the obliques, and how fast it tired helped predict how long they lasted (Juan-Recio and colleagues, 2022)
Back musclesThey help hold the line: 28% of their maximum in the women, and up to 46% when rolling in and out (Youdas and colleagues, 2014)

A 2007 study of 30 people found the side bridge strong enough to build the gluteus medius and the external oblique, both working above the 45% of maximum the authors linked with strength gains (Ekstrom and colleagues, 2007).

Side plank benefits: what holds up

It builds the side of your core and hipYes. The obliques and the gluteus medius work hard enough to get stronger, in three separate muscle studies
It works your abs without the hip flexorsYes. It trained the ab wall best while asking little of the psoas, the deep hip flexor that sit-ups work hard. Spine researchers list it with the curl-up and the bird dog as spine stabilization exercises (McGill and Karpowicz, 2009)
It prevents injuriesNot shown. In 140 college athletes, only the strength of turning the hip outward predicted who got hurt (Leetun and colleagues, 2004). In 75 runners and jumpers, side plank times had no link with overuse injuries (Luedke and Rauh, 2022)
It fixes scoliosisNot shown. A study without a control group reported smaller curves, a randomized trial found no change. Details below
It burns love handlesNo. No exercise takes fat off one spot: six weeks of ab exercises left belly fat as it was in a 2011 study (Vispute and colleagues, 2011)

Does the side plank help scoliosis?

The claim comes from a 2014 report on 25 people aged 14 to 85 with scoliosis, a sideways curve of the spine. They held a side plank once a day for as long as they could, only on the side where the curve bulges, facing down. After 6.8 months on average, their main curve was 32% smaller, and 41% smaller in the 19 who kept it up. There was no comparison group (Fishman and colleagues, 2014).

A randomized trial then tested it in 64 teenagers aged 10 to 17, comparing the side plank with a front plank. Only 22 kept it up four times a week or more, and after 6 months neither group's curves had changed (Sarkisova and colleagues, 2019). The author of the first study replied that the trial did not test the curves the side plank is meant for, those in the lower back, and that it may make a curve higher up the back worse (Fishman, 2021).

So it is not proven, and it may not suit every curve. Scoliosis needs a doctor who treats it: ask before you use side planks for it.

The Copenhagen plank

The Copenhagen plank, or Copenhagen adduction exercise, is a side plank on your elbow with your top leg resting on a bench or held by a partner, so the inner thigh of that leg takes your weight. With the knee on the support it is easier, with the ankle on it harder (Marušič and colleagues, 2026).

In the biggest trial, 652 male football players on 35 Norwegian teams did it or trained as usual. The Copenhagen group did it three times a week before the season and once a week during it. In an average week of the season, 13.5% of them reported groin problems, against 21.3% of the others: a 41% lower risk (Harøy and colleagues, 2019). But a 2025 review that pooled the three trials of the exercise on its own found no clear drop in groin problems, with very low quality evidence. It did find large gains in inner thigh strength (Quintana-Cepedal and colleagues, 2025).

So it builds the inner thigh, and it may help footballers' groins. Start with the knee on the bench. RankTime does not time it: its camera knows the side plank with both feet on the floor.

A 4 week side plank plan

Test both sides first, then hold for time, not to the limit, three days a week. The American College of Sports Medicine advises at least two sessions a week for strength (Currier and colleagues, 2026).

  • Day 1: test. Hold each side until your hips drop, with a few minutes of rest in between. Write both times down.
  • Your hold: half your weaker side's time. Both sides hold for that long.
  • Each session: 3 rounds of left, then right, with 15 seconds of rest after each side.
  • Each week: add 5 seconds to the hold.
  • End of week 2 and week 4: test again, and set your new hold.
Your test under 30 s3 rounds of 10 to 15 seconds a side, on your knees if you need to

Common questions

Is a side plank better than a regular plank?

Neither: they work different sides of your core. In the 75 college athletes, the front plank lasted 91 seconds on average and each side plank 75. Do both: the PLANKS workout in RankTime runs the front, side and reverse plank.

Why is one side weaker?

It usually is, a little: 10 seconds apart on average in the college study, and the test itself wobbles by about 11 seconds from one week to the next.

Why does my shoulder give out first?

Your bottom shoulder carries much of your weight. In the 2022 study of 24 women, the shoulder muscle tired as fast as the obliques. If it hurts, use the knee version.

How many times a week?

Two or three, as in the plan. The ACSM advises at least two strength sessions a week.

Will side planks get rid of love handles?

No exercise burns fat from one spot. Side planks build the muscle underneath. Whether it shows depends on the fat over it.

Does RankTime count side plank hip dips?

No. It times the side plank as a hold. Dip your hips and the clock stops until you are straight again.
