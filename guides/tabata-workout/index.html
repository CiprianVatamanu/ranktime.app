Tabata Workout: The 4 Minute 20/10 Protocol Explained

Updated October 7, 2026 · 6 minute read

Tabata is the shortest real workout you can do: four minutes, start to finish. The catch is that those four minutes are meant to be as hard as you can make them. Here is how it works, which exercises suit it, and how to run one.

What is a Tabata workout?

Work20 seconds, all out
Rest10 seconds
Rounds8
Total time4 minutes

That is the whole protocol. One round is 30 seconds, so eight rounds take exactly four minutes. Because the rest is only half as long as the work, you never fully recover between rounds, and that is the point.

Where Tabata comes from

The protocol is named after Dr. Izumi Tabata, a Japanese researcher who published a study in 1996 with athletes on exercise bikes. Riders who did eight rounds of 20 seconds at very high intensity with 10 seconds of rest, several times a week, improved both their endurance and their ability to work at full power. The original sessions were brutally hard: close to maximum effort on every round.

Most "Tabata" classes today are easier than the original. That is fine for fitness, just know that the real thing means going as hard as you can for every 20 seconds.

Best exercises for Tabata

Pick moves that are simple, safe to do fast, and get your heart rate up. Good choices:

  • Air squats or jump squats
  • Push-ups
  • Burpees
  • Mountain climbers
  • Kettlebell swings
  • Bike, rower or sprints, the closest match to the original study

Avoid anything technical or heavy, like heavy barbell lifts. Form breaks down fast when you are tired, and Tabata makes you tired on purpose.

Sample Tabata workouts

Beginner, 4 minutes

8 rounds of air squats. Count your reps each round and try to keep the number steady from round one to round eight.

Intermediate, 8 minutes

Two Tabatas back to back with one minute of rest between them: squats first, then push-ups.

Advanced, 16 minutes

Four Tabatas with one minute of rest between each: squats, push-ups, burpees, mountain climbers.

Common Tabata mistakes

  • Pacing yourself. If you could keep going after eight rounds, you went too easy. Each round should feel like a sprint.
  • Watching the clock. Use a timer that beeps or shows color, so your eyes stay on your form.
  • Skipping the warm up. Five minutes of easy movement before you start lowers the risk of a strain.
  • Doing it every day. Real all out efforts need recovery. Two or three sessions a week is plenty.
