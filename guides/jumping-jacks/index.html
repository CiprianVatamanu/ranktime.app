Jumping Jacks: Calories Burned, Benefits and How Many to Do

Updated November 26, 2026 · 13 minute read · Free PDF

Jumping jacks are a whole-body cardio exercise: you jump your feet apart as your arms swing up over your head, then jump back. They count as vigorous exercise, and a 70 kg (154 lb) adult burns about 9 calories a minute doing them, so 100 calories takes about 11 minutes of non-stop jacks. Here are the numbers for your weight, how to do them well, what studies found they do for your fitness, knees and bones, and how many to do.

How many calories do jumping jacks burn?

About 7.5 times what your body burns at rest. The 2024 Compendium of Physical Activities, the reference list researchers use for the energy cost of activities, puts vigorous calisthenics, jumping jacks named among them, at 7.5 METs (Herrmann and colleagues, 2024). One MET is about 1 calorie per kilogram of body weight per hour, so a minute of jumping jacks burns about an eighth of a calorie for every kilogram you weigh.

How do you do jumping jacks properly?

  1. Stand tall, feet together, arms by your sides.
  2. Jump out. Hop just a little off the floor and land with your feet wider than your shoulders, while your arms swing out to the sides and up over your head. Arms go up as the legs go out.
  3. Jump back in. Feet together, arms back down by your sides. That is one jumping jack.
  4. Keep your back straight and your stomach tight the whole time.
  5. Hands apart at the top: they should not cross over your head.
  6. Keep a steady rhythm, a moderate pace you can hold.

These are the steps of the US military's version, the side straddle hop, as a military college's training manual writes them (below).

How do you do low impact jumping jacks?

Step instead of jumping. Step one foot out to the side as both arms swing up, bring it back as they come down, then do the other side. One foot stays on the floor the whole time, so there is no landing and no thud: the version for sore knees, a heavy body, or a flat with neighbours below. In a pool it is gentler still: with the head out of the water, six exercises, jumping jacks among them, landed with 28 to 59% of the force they did on dry land (Alberton and colleagues, 2015).

What is a side straddle hop?

The US military's name for the jumping jack. The Citadel, the military college of South Carolina, has it as exercise 3 of the preparation drill in its training manual: start at attention; count 1, jump slightly into the air, legs more than shoulder width apart, arms swinging out and up overhead; count 2, back to the start; counts 3 and 4, the same again, at a moderate cadence. Its purpose, the manual says, is low level plyometric movement and upper body range of motion.

Are jumping jacks good cardio?

Yes. At 7.5 METs they are vigorous exercise, which starts at 6 METs (Herrmann and colleagues, 2024). And a few minutes of them can raise your fitness:

4 minutes vs 3022 active young women trained 4 days a week for 4 weeks. One group trained 30 minutes on a treadmill at about 85% of maximum heart rate. Another did 8 rounds of 20 seconds of one exercise, jumping jacks, burpees, mountain climbers or squat thrusts, with 10 seconds of rest: 4 minutes. Aerobic fitness rose about 8% with the 4 minutes and 7% with the 30, and only the 4 minute group got better muscle endurance, push-ups up 135% (McRae and colleagues, 2012)
Children and teensIn 106 young people aged 6 to 18, jumping jacks cost the most energy of the 15 activities measured, from watching a film to fast walking on a treadmill (Schuna and colleagues, 2016)
Every minute countsThe World Health Organization advises adults 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity a week, or 150 to 300 of moderate. Since 2020, bouts of any length count toward it: the old 10 minute minimum is gone (Bull and colleagues, 2020)

The 4 minutes in that study are a Tabata workout: 20 seconds on, 10 off, 8 rounds. RankTime has one ready, below.

Do jumping jacks help you lose weight?

A little, and slowly, like any exercise on its own. The American College of Sports Medicine reviewed the research: 150 to 250 minutes a week of moderate activity prevents weight gain but brings only modest weight loss, more than 250 minutes a week was linked with clinically significant loss, and in studies that also cut calories moderately, the activity added to the loss (Donnelly and colleagues, 2009). In jumping jacks, 500 calories is about an hour for a 70 kg person.

Do jumping jacks burn belly fat?

Not from your belly first: no exercise burns the fat over the muscle it works. In a 2011 study, 24 inactive adults did seven ab exercises five days a week for 6 weeks, and their belly fat and waists did not change (Vispute and colleagues, 2011). Hard intervals do cut belly fat as part of losing fat overall: in a 2018 review of 39 interval training studies with 617 people, intervals reduced total fat, belly fat and the deep visceral fat around the organs (Maillard and colleagues, 2018). What muscles do jumping jacks work?

Mostly your heart and lungs. For the muscles, no study has measured which ones work in a jumping jack: we searched the research on 26 November 2026 and found none. This table comes from what each muscle does, as the OpenStax anatomy textbook describes it.

Legs outThe muscles on the side of your hip, the gluteus medius and minimus, which move the leg out to the side
Legs inThe adductors on the inside of your thighs, which bring the legs back together
Every hopYour calves, which point the foot and push you off the floor
Arms upThe deltoids, the main muscles that lift your arms out to the side
Arms downThe latissimus dorsi on your back and the chest muscles, which bring your arms back to your sides
The whole timeYour stomach muscles, holding your back straight, as the military's version asks

Do jumping jacks build muscle?

Not much on their own. Each jack asks little of any one muscle, which is why they count as cardio. The muscle endurance gains in the 2012 study came from a mix of jumping jacks, burpees, mountain climbers and squat thrusts. For strength, add exercises that load each muscle more, such as push-ups, squats or lunges.

Are jumping jacks bad for your knees?

Not for most people, but they are an impact exercise: every landing loads your legs with more than your body weight. Children on force plates landed at 2.6 times their body weight in a jumping jack in one study and 3.5 times in another, and jumping down from a box landed harder in both (Fassett and colleagues, 2022; McKay and colleagues, 2005). If your knees, shins or feet complain, do the low impact version above, or your jacks in a pool.

Are jumping jacks good for bone density?

They are a gentle jump, and bone responds to jumping. The 2005 force plate study measured the jumps of a school program that improved children's bone health, and in a 2006 study, 10 maximal vertical jumps a day, 3 days a week, raised young women's bone density at the hip by 2.6% and in the spine by 2.4% in 6 months (McKay and colleagues, 2005; Kato and colleagues, 2006). Those were bigger jumps than jacks. In the 2022 study every jump stayed under 3 times body weight except jumping down from a box, and its authors suggested that may not be enough to improve bone (Fassett and colleagues, 2022). For bone, the programs that worked used bigger jumps.

Why do I pee when I do jumping jacks?

It is common, and treatable. In a survey of 59 active women in Canada, skipping, trampolines, jumping jacks and running were the activities most likely to cause leaks, and half said leaking held back how much they exercised (Brennand and colleagues, 2018). The first treatment is pelvic floor muscle training: in a Cochrane review of 31 trials, women with stress urinary incontinence, leaks when you jump, cough or sneeze, who trained were 8 times more likely to report a cure, 56% against 6% (Dumoulin and colleagues, 2018). A pelvic floor physiotherapist can teach it. Until then, step jacks have no landing.

Values from the 2024 Compendium of Physical Activities. Jumping jacks match jogging minute for minute, and a skipping rope at a steady pace burns about half as much again. Measured in a lab, short sets of jumping jacks cost about as much energy as push-ups and squats, twice as much as a plank, and less than burpees, which cost the most of seven bodyweight exercises (Poulios and colleagues, 2024). What jumping jacks need is less: no rope, no road, and a square metre of floor.

How many jumping jacks should you do a day?

No study has set a number. The World Health Organization's 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity a week works out at 11 to 21 minutes a day, or 25 to 50 minutes on three days, and since 2020 short bouts count. Jumping jacks can be part of that, next to anything else that gets you breathing hard. To get fitter with them, the 2012 study is a good model: 4 minutes, 4 days a week. Here it is as a 4 week plan.

Common questions

Can you do jumping jacks every day?

Yes, if your body is happy with it: the World Health Organization's targets are weekly totals, and every day's minutes add to them. Your feet, shins and knees take the landings, so if they ache, switch to step jacks or rest a day. To get fitter, 4 days a week was enough in the 2012 study.

Are jumping jacks a good warm-up?

Yes. The Citadel's training manual puts the side straddle hop in its preparation drill, the warm-up before training: 5 to 10 repetitions at a moderate pace.

Can you do jumping jacks while pregnant?

The World Health Organization says women who were active before pregnancy, including those who did vigorous activity, can carry on during pregnancy and after it (Bull and colleagues, 2020). Ask your midwife or doctor about your own pregnancy.

Are star jumps the same as jumping jacks?

In British English, yes: star jump is the UK name for the jumping jack. The same name is also used for a jump where you spread into a star shape in the air and land with your feet together (Cambridge Dictionary).

Why are they called jumping jacks?

We could not find a source that says for sure. A jumping jack is also an old toy, a little figure whose arms and legs jump when you pull its string, and the exercise moves just like it. The Cambridge Dictionary lists both.

Do jumping jacks help lymphatic drainage?

We found no study that measured it. A search of the research on 26 November 2026 found none on jumping jacks, rebounding or trampolines and lymph flow.
