The Cindy Workout: 20 Minute AMRAP Guide

Updated November 3, 2026 · 5 minute read

Cindy is one of the best known benchmark workouts there is: three bodyweight moves, 20 minutes, as many rounds as you can. All it needs is a pull-up bar. This guide covers what it is, what a good score looks like, how to scale it and how to pace it.

What is the Cindy workout?

As many rounds as possible in 20 minutes of:

5pull-ups
10push-ups
15air squats
ScoreRounds plus extra reps, for example 14 rounds + 8 reps

One round is 30 reps. After every round you start again at the pull-ups, and you keep going until the 20 minutes are up. It is an AMRAP: the clock is fixed, the work is up to you.

Where Cindy comes from

Cindy is one of the Girls, the benchmark workouts CrossFit published in its early years and gave women's names. A benchmark is a workout you repeat every few months to see how far you have come, and Cindy is one of the most popular because it needs so little: a bar, the floor and 20 minutes. Its 5, 10, 15 pattern is also the most common way to split the reps of Murph, which is 20 rounds of it between two miles.

What is a good Cindy score?

A rough guide, unweighted, with full reps:

  • Under 10 rounds: a fine start.
  • 10 to 15 rounds: solid fitness.
  • 15 to 20 rounds: strong, especially if your pull-ups hold up to the end.
  • Over 20 rounds: faster than a round a minute. Very fit.

The score that matters most is your own last one. Beat that.

How to scale Cindy

  • Easier pull-ups. Band assisted pull-ups, jumping pull-ups, or ring rows.
  • Easier push-ups. Hands on a bench or box, or from your knees.
  • A shorter clock. 10 or 15 minutes instead of 20.
  • Half rounds. 3 pull-ups, 6 push-ups, 9 squats, for the same 20 minutes.
  • Harder. Wear a weight vest, the way many people do Murph.

Pacing tips

  1. Know your round time. 20 rounds means a round a minute. Divide 20 minutes by the rounds you are aiming for and hold that pace from the start.
  2. Watch the push-ups. They usually fail before the pull-ups do. Split them early, 6 and 4 or 5 and 5, before they get slow.
  3. Do not rest at the bar. Jump up, do the 5, move on. If you need a breather, take it after the squats.
  4. Use the squats to recover. Keep them steady and breathe.
