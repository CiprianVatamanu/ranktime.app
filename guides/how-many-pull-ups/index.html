How Many Pull-Ups Should You Be Able to Do?

Updated November 14, 2026 · 7 minute read · Free PDF

Ask ten people how many pull-ups is good and you will get ten answers. There is no official standard for adults, but there are clear benchmarks: the levels coaches use, the numbers the military asks for, and your own best set. Here is what each number means, how to test yours fairly, and how to make it go up.

The short answer

For men, 10 strict pull-ups is a strong, realistic goal, 5 is a good start, and 20 or more is elite. For women, the first strict pull-up is a real milestone, 3 to 5 is fit, and 10 or more is very strong. If you cannot do one yet, you are in good company: plenty of adults who never train their back cannot, and most get there with a few weeks of the right practice.

Pull-up standards by level

These levels are for strict pull-ups in one set: from a dead hang, palms facing away, chin over the bar, no swinging. They are a rough guide drawn from military tables and common coaching benchmarks, not an official norm, and body weight moves them a lot, because a heavier person lifts more with every rep.

Not yet0. Normal if you have never trained pulling. Start with hangs, rows and slow negatives from our pull-up progression.
BeginnerMen 1 to 4, women 1 to 2. The hardest step is behind you. Short, frequent sets add reps quickly from here.
FitMen 5 to 9, women 3 to 5. Enough to train pull-ups properly: real sets, ladders and EMOMs.
StrongMen 10 to 14, women 6 to 9. A common long term goal. Ten is also the minimum the Navy asks of SEAL candidates.
Very strongMen 15 to 19, women 10 to 14. Few people get here without months of training pull-ups on purpose.
EliteMen 20 or more, women 15 or more. Full marks on the Marine Corps fitness test are 23 for men and 12 for women at their peak ages.

The number that matters most is your own from a few weeks ago. Beat that, and your training is working.

How many pull-ups can the average person do?

There is no large official survey of adults, so any average you read online is an estimate. Many of them come from people who already log their gym training, which pushes them up. What coaches agree on: a lot of adults who do not train cannot do a single strict pull-up, especially at a higher body weight, and that is a starting point, not a verdict. Pull-ups respond quickly to practice, so the gap between average and good is usually a few months of regular training, not talent.

Does age change the number?

Less than you might think. Strength fades slowly, and the military tables show it. These are the pull-ups that earn full marks, 100 points, on the US Marine Corps fitness test:

Age 17 to 2020 for men, 7 for women
Age 21 to 2523 for men, 11 for women
Age 26 to 3023 for men, 12 for women
Age 31 to 3523 for men, 11 for women
Age 36 to 4021 for men, 10 for women
Age 41 to 4520 for men, 8 for women
Age 46 to 5019 for men, 6 for women
Age 51 and over18 for men, 4 for women

A fit 50 year old man is expected to do almost as many as a 25 year old. If your number has dropped over the years, training more often usually brings much of it back.

Military pull-up standards

Pull-ups are less common in military tests than push-ups. Here is where they count:

US Marine CorpsPull-ups are one of three events in the yearly fitness test, with a plank and a 3 mile run. The minimum is 3 to 5 for men and 1 to 4 for women, depending on age, and full marks are in the table above. Marines may do push-ups instead, but push-ups score at most 70 of the 100 points, so most choose pull-ups.
Navy SEALsThe screening test asks for at least 10 pull-ups, with no time limit. Competitive candidates do 15 to 20 or more.
US ArmyNo pull-ups. The Army Fitness Test is a deadlift, hand-release push-ups, a sprint-drag-carry, a plank and a 2 mile run.
Navy and Air ForceNo pull-ups in the standard fitness tests.

Standards change every few years, so check the current rules for the exact test you will take.

How to test your max pull-ups

  1. Test fresh. At the start of a session, after a light warm up: a 20 second hang and a couple of easy or band assisted reps.
  2. Start from a dead hang. Palms facing away, hands just wider than your shoulders, arms fully straight, feet off the floor.
  3. Chin over the bar on every rep, then lower all the way back to straight arms. A rep that stops halfway is not a rep.
  4. No swinging or kipping. A kick of the legs lets your hips do the work. Keep your legs still and slightly in front of you.
  5. Stop when the form stops. You may hang and breathe at the bottom between reps. The set ends when you let go, or when a rep stalls below the bar.
  6. Write it down with the date and your grip. Test again in a week or two, not every day.

Counting is the weak point of most tests. Halfway through a hard set it is easy to lose count, or to count a rep that did not quite reach the bar. A partner helps, or a camera.

How many pull-ups should you do a day?

To get better, about two to three times your max a day is plenty, split into easy sets of around half your max. If your best set is 6, that is 12 to 18 pull-ups a day in sets of 3, with a rest day or two a week. Never go to failure on these: the idea is lots of clean reps while you are fresh.

Max 1 to 3Singles: 3 to 6 single reps with a full rest between them, plus a few slow negatives.
Max 4 to 8Sets of 2 to 4. An EMOM works well: one set at the start of every minute for 10 minutes.
Max 9 to 15Sets of 4 to 7, or a ladder: 1, 2, 3, 4 reps, then start again at 1.
Max 15 or moreAdd weight with a backpack or a vest, or use pull-ups in longer workouts like Cindy and Murph.

If the inside of your elbow starts to ache, cut back for a week and switch to chin-ups. Tendons adapt more slowly than muscles. Pull-ups pair well with push-ups, too: see the push-up test for that side.
