EMOM Workout: What It Means and How to Do It

Updated October 7, 2026 · 6 minute read

EMOM stands for every minute on the minute. It is one of the simplest ways to structure a workout, and one of the most honest: the clock decides when you start, and how fast you work decides how much rest you get.

How an EMOM works

At the start of every minute, you do a set number of reps. When you finish, you rest until the next minute begins. Then you go again.

Example10 push-ups every minute for 10 minutes
If your set takes 20 sYou rest 40 s
If your set takes 45 sYou rest only 15 s
Total100 push-ups in 10 minutes

The faster you move, the more you rest. As you tire, your sets slow down and your rest shrinks, so the workout gets harder on its own without you changing anything.

Why EMOM works so well

  • Built in pacing. You cannot start too fast and burn out, because the clock holds you back until the next minute.
  • Lots of quality reps. Small sets with rest in between let you do more total work with good form than one long set.
  • Easy to track progress. Same EMOM next week with one more rep per minute, or two more minutes, and you know exactly how much you improved.
  • Fits any exercise. Bodyweight, kettlebells, barbells, even skill work like pull-up practice.

How to pick the right number of reps

The rule of thumb: your set should take 20 to 40 seconds, leaving at least 20 seconds of rest. If you cannot finish a set inside the minute, the reps are too high. Drop them and keep going rather than stopping.

A simple starting point is about half of what you can do in one all out set. If your max is 20 push-ups, start an EMOM at 10.

Sample EMOM workouts

Beginner, 10 minutes

Every minute: 10 air squats. That is 100 squats, done with plenty of rest.

Alternating, 12 minutes

Odd minutes: 8 push-ups. Even minutes: 12 squats. Six rounds of each, and each muscle group gets two minutes between sets.

Pull-up builder, 20 minutes

Every minute: 2 to 5 pull-ups. Small, crisp sets add up to 40 to 100 pull-ups, more than most people manage any other way.

Three move, 15 minutes

Minute 1: 10 kettlebell swings. Minute 2: 10 push-ups. Minute 3: 15 squats. Repeat 5 times.

EMOM vs Tabata vs AMRAP

  • EMOM: fixed reps each minute, rest is whatever is left over.
  • Tabata: fixed time, 20 seconds of work and 10 of rest, as many reps as you can. See our Tabata guide.
  • AMRAP: as many rounds or reps as possible in a set time, with rest only when you choose.
