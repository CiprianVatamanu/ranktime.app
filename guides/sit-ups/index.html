Sit-Ups: Good or Bad? What the Research Says and How Many to Do

Updated November 23, 2026 · 13 minute read · Free PDF

A sit-up takes you from lying on your back, knees bent, to sitting up with your elbows on your knees, and back down. It works the long muscle down the front of your belly and the hip flexors that pull your chest up to your thighs. Sit-ups have a bad name, but the largest trial found no sign that they hurt healthy backs. Soldiers did 5 minutes of either sit-ups and crunches or other core exercises, five days a week for 12 weeks, and over the next two years 17.5% and 17.6% of them sought care for low back pain. What sit-ups will not do is burn belly fat. Here is what the research says, how to do a sit-up that counts, sit-ups vs crunches, how many you should manage in a minute at your age, and a 6 week plan.

Sit-ups: six claims checked

Six things people say about sit-ups, and what the studies found. The sections below have the detail, and the sources are at the end.

Sit-ups hurt your backNo sign of it in healthy backs. Over two years, 17.5% of soldiers on a sit-up routine and 17.6% on other core exercises sought care for low back pain
Sit-ups burn belly fatNo. Six weeks of ab exercises, five days a week, left belly fat and waist size where they were
Bent knees take the hip flexors outNo. The bent-knee sit-up was among the moves that worked the hip flexor on the front of the thigh hardest, and every sit-up tested worked the deep one, the psoas, at 15 to 35% of its maximum
Crunches work your abs betterPartly. The abs worked through 90% of a crunch but only 34% of a straight-leg sit-up. A sit-up asks more of the hip flexors
Sit-ups are a waste of timeNo. In 8,116 Canadians followed for 13 years, those with the lowest sit-up scores died at about 2.3 to 2.7 times the rate of those with the highest. A link, not proof
Sit-ups suit everyoneNot with osteoporosis. Bending exercises went with far more new spine fractures in women with fragile spines, and bone health guidelines say to avoid bending the spine under load

Do sit-ups hurt your back?

The worry is the bending. A sit-up curls your lower back forward while your muscles pull hard on it. A crunch, which lifts only your shoulder blades, bent the lower back about 3 degrees in a 1979 study, against 38 degrees for a full sit-up (Halpern and Bleck, 1979). When spine researchers measured 12 ab exercises in nine volunteers, no single one trained all the ab muscles while keeping the load on the spine low, and partial curl-ups gave the most ab work for the least load (Axler and McGill, 1997).

Whether that hurts real backs is a different question. The US Army tested it on thousands of soldiers, comparing a sit-up routine with core stability exercises that left sit-ups out:

Back pain over 2 years4,325 soldiers with no history of back pain trained 5 minutes a day, five days a week, for 12 weeks. 17.5% of the sit-up group and 17.6% of the core group later sought care for low back pain (George and colleagues, 2011)
Injuries in training1,141 soldiers over 16 weeks: 44.8% had an injury that limited their duties, with no difference between the groups. Those who hurt their low back lost more days in the sit-up group, 8.3 against 4.2, a difference the authors call marginal (Childs and colleagues, 2010)
Test scores2,616 soldiers, four sessions a week for 12 weeks: both groups raised their sit-up scores, with no difference between them, and the core group's pass rate rose a little more, 5.6% against 3.9% (Childs and colleagues, 2009)

So for a back without problems, sit-ups showed no extra risk. Two groups should be careful:

  • Osteoporosis or a fragile spine. In a small 1984 study of 59 women with spinal osteoporosis and back pain, 89% of those given bending exercises had new spine fractures within one to six years, against 16% of those given back extension exercises (Sinaki and Mikkelsen, 1984). The Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation advises people with low bone density or osteoporosis to avoid bending the spine forward under load (LeBoff and colleagues, 2022).
  • Back pain right now. If sit-ups bring it on, stop and see a doctor or physiotherapist. Two moves are gentler: the curl-up gave the most ab work for the least spine load in the 1997 study, and the side plank the most ab work with the least psoas in a 1998 one (Juker and colleagues, 1998).

Do sit-ups burn belly fat?

No, not on their own. In a 2011 study, 24 inactive adults either did seven ab exercises, 2 sets of 10 reps five days a week for 6 weeks, or no exercise, and everyone kept eating the same. After 6 weeks the ab exercises had changed nothing in body weight, body fat, belly fat, waist size or skinfolds. They did build endurance: the ab group did 47 curl-ups in the final test, against 32 for the group that did none (Vispute and colleagues, 2011).

The calories are small too. The 2024 Compendium of Physical Activities rates calisthenics such as sit-ups at 3.8 METs at a moderate effort and 7.5 at a vigorous one. A MET is about 1 calorie per kilogram of body weight per hour, so 10 minutes of sit-ups burns about 45 calories at a steady pace for a 70 kg (154 lb) person, and under 90 at a hard one.

Sit-ups build the muscle under your belly fat. Whether it shows depends on the fat over it, and the sit-ups themselves do not change that.

Sit-ups vs crunches

A crunch lifts your head and shoulder blades off the floor and stops there. A sit-up keeps going until your elbows touch your knees. In the studies above, they compare like this:

Sit-upCrunch
How highElbows to kneesShoulder blades up
Lower back bendAbout 38 degreesAbout 3 degrees
Abs at work34% of a straight-leg sit-up90% of the movement
Deep hip flexor15 to 35% of its maximumUnder 10% in a curl-up
Pick it forA test that counts sit-upsAb work with little back bending

Training for a test that counts sit-ups? Train sit-ups: the test counts nothing else. Want the most ab work for the least bending of your back? Crunches and curl-ups do that. And since no single exercise trained every ab muscle in the 1997 study, mix them: sit-ups or crunches for the front, a side plank for the sides.

Sit-ups muscles worked

Rectus abdominisThe long muscle down the front of your belly, the six-pack. It curls your chest toward your hips
ObliquesAt the sides of your waist. They help curl you up and keep your trunk steady
PsoasThe deep hip flexor, from your lower spine to your thigh bone. It pulls your trunk up to your thighs: 15 to 35% of its maximum in every sit-up tested, under 10% in a curl-up
Rectus femorisThe hip flexor on the front of your thigh. The bent-knee sit-up was among the moves that worked it hardest

That hip flexor work is what makes a sit-up feel different from a crunch, and the researchers who measured it note that it may be a problem for some people with low back trouble (Escamilla and colleagues, 2006).

How to do a sit-up

This is the sit-up that police fitness tests count, as the New York State Police and Pennsylvania's police academies describe it:

  1. Lie down. On your back, knees bent to about a right angle, heels flat on the floor, feet together or apart.
  2. Hands. Lace your fingers behind your head, not on top of it or on your neck, and keep them laced. Keep your head in line with your body: never pull on it.
  3. Feet. A partner holds them down firmly. At home, hook them under something heavy, like a sofa.
  4. Up. Curl your upper body up until your elbows touch your knees. Keep breathing normally.
  5. Down. Lower until your shoulder blades touch the floor. That is one rep: it counts when you are back down.
  6. Hips down. Keep your hips flat on the floor and do not arch your back.

How many sit-ups can you do in a minute?

The 1 minute sit-up test is the one police forces still use: as many correct sit-ups as you can in 60 seconds, done exactly as above, with someone holding your feet. The lines below are the Cooper Institute's norms, which the New York State Park Police and Pennsylvania's police academies use, and the New York State Police publish the same 50% and 70% lines. 50% means you did more than half of people your age and sex.

What police ask for: Pennsylvania's academies take recruits at the 30% line, which is 17 for men and 4 for women over 60. The New York State Park Police ask for the 40% line to pass their entry test, 38 for men in their twenties and 32 for women, and the 70% line to graduate, 45 and 41.

Test yourself after a warm up, with a partner holding your feet and a clock that stops you at 60 seconds. Count only reps where your elbows touch your knees and your shoulder blades touch the floor. If you need a breather, take it sitting up: Pennsylvania's test ends for anyone who rests lying down.

How many sit-ups should you do a day?

No study has tested a number of sit-ups a day. What researchers tested were short routines, and they worked:

  • Soldiers, 5 minutes a day. The Army trial's sit-up routine was five 1 minute drills, five days a week, for 12 weeks (George and colleagues, 2011). Four sessions a week for 12 weeks raised sit-up scores (Childs and colleagues, 2009).
  • Beginners, 2 sets of 10. Inactive adults who did 2 sets of 10 of seven ab exercises, five days a week, managed 47 curl-ups after 6 weeks, against 32 for those who did none (Vispute and colleagues, 2011).
  • Twice a week or more. The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 review of the evidence found that strength training improves muscular endurance, and that two or more sessions a week build strength. It found too little research to say which number of reps or sets builds endurance best (Currier and colleagues, 2026).

So skip the big daily number. About 5 minutes, four or five days a week, with a day or two off, is what raised scores in the trials. A 1 minute test every few weeks tells you whether it is working for you.

The soldiers' 5 minute sit-up routine and a 6 week plan

This is the sit-up routine from the Army trial: one minute of each, back to back (George and colleagues, 2011).

Minute 1Sit-ups
Minute 2Sit-ups with a twist to the left
Minute 3Sit-ups with a twist to the right
Minute 4Crunches
Minute 5Sit-ups

The trials also tested a core stability routine with no sit-ups in it: 5 minutes of moves, a minute each, among them side planks on both sides, a shoulder bridge and a bird dog (on hands and knees, one arm and the other leg reaching out). It raised sit-up scores as much as sit-ups did (Childs and colleagues, 2009). Use it on days your back wants less bending.

A 6 week plan.

  • Day 1: test. Warm up, then the 1 minute test. Write down your score and find it in the tables above.
  • Monday to Friday: the 5 minute routine. Rest at the weekend. Each week, aim for a few more clean reps in each minute.
  • End of week 3 and end of week 6: test again.

Six weeks was enough to raise ab endurance in the 2011 study, and the Army trials ran 12: keep going while your score climbs. For more on short timed sessions, see our guide to the EMOM workout.

Common questions

Should someone hold your feet?

For a test, yes: police tests hold them, so practise that way. At home, hook them under a sofa.

Bent knees or straight legs?

Bent, as police tests do it. Bending your knees does not switch off the hip flexors, though: the bent-knee sit-up worked the one on the front of the thigh among the hardest in a 2006 study.

Are planks better than sit-ups?

They train different things: a sit-up moves your spine, a plank holds it still. The Army, Navy and Marines all test the plank now, and in the 1998 study of deep muscle activity, the side plank trained the ab wall best while asking little of the deep hip flexor. For plank times, see average plank time by age.

Can you do sit-ups every day?

The Army's routine ran five days a week, and so did the 2011 beginners' study. Nothing in the research needs all seven.

How long until you can do more sit-ups?

Six weeks raised ab endurance in the 2011 study, and the Army trials ran 12 weeks. Test every 3 weeks to watch your number move.

Will sit-ups give you a six-pack?

They build the muscle, but six weeks of ab work left belly fat unchanged in the 2011 study. Whether the muscle shows depends on the fat over it.
