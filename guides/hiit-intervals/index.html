HIIT Intervals: How to Set Work and Rest Times

Updated October 19, 2026 · 7 minute read

Every HIIT workout comes down to three numbers: how long you work, how long you rest, and how many rounds you do. Change them and the same exercises turn into a very different workout. Here is how to pick them for your goal.

The three numbers

WorkHow long each hard effort lasts
RestHow long you recover before the next one
RoundsHow many times the pair repeats
Total timeWork plus rest, times rounds. 40 seconds on and 20 off for 10 rounds is 10 minutes.

Of the three, the ratio between work and rest matters most. It decides how much you recover between efforts, and that decides how hard each effort can be.

Work to rest ratios explained

2:1, like 40/20 or 20/10Rest is half the work. You never fully recover, so your heart rate keeps climbing from round to round. Hard, and great for conditioning. Tabata is the best known 2:1 workout.
1:1, like 30/30 or 45/45Equal work and rest. Hard but sustainable, so your form holds up over a longer session. A good default for most people.
1:2 to 1:3, like 20/40 or 15/45Short work, long rest. You recover almost fully, so every effort can be close to all out. Best for sprints and power, and the friendliest ratio for beginners.
EMOMWork and rest share a fixed minute: the faster you finish the work, the more rest you get. The EMOM guide covers it in full.

Which ratio fits your goal

  • Just starting out: 1:2. Try 20 seconds on and 40 off. You learn to push hard without falling apart after three rounds.
  • General fitness and calorie burn: 1:1 to 2:1. 30/30 or 40/20 with bodyweight moves keeps your heart rate high for the whole session.
  • Speed and power: 1:3 or more. Short, all out sprints of 10 to 15 seconds with 30 to 60 seconds of rest. If you cannot hold full speed, rest longer.
  • Endurance: longer intervals. Efforts of 2 to 4 minutes with 1 to 3 minutes of easy movement in between. The best known is the 4x4: four minutes hard, three minutes easy, four times.

How many rounds, and how long?

For most people, 10 to 20 minutes of intervals is plenty, after a 5 minute warm up. Watch your last rounds: if they are much weaker than your first ones, you did too many rounds or rested too little for the ratio you picked.

Two or three HIIT sessions a week is enough. Hard intervals need recovery, and doing them every day usually makes every session worse.

Sample interval sessions

Beginner: 20/40, 10 rounds, 10 minutes

Alternate air squats and incline push-ups. Go easy on round one, then hold a steady, hard effort.

Intermediate: 40/20, 10 rounds, 10 minutes

Squats, push-ups, lunges, mountain climbers and burpees, twice through. Aim to keep the same pace in round ten as in round one.

Sprints: 15/45, 8 rounds, 8 minutes

All out on a bike, a rower or a hill, with easy pedaling or walking in the rest. Stop early if your speed drops off.

Endurance: 4x4, 28 minutes

Four minutes of running, rowing or cycling, hard enough that you can only speak a few words, then three minutes of easy movement. Four rounds.

Let your heart rate tell you

A heart rate strap shows whether your ratio is doing its job.

  • Work should reach the hard zones. In most HIIT sessions your heart rate should climb above about 80% of your maximum within the first few rounds. Short sprints with long rests are the exception: they are about speed, not heart rate.
  • Watch it next to your reps. If your heart rate barely drops during rest and your reps are falling apart, rest longer or cut a round.
  • Estimate your maximum. 208 minus 0.7 times your age is a better rough guess than the old 220 minus your age. For a 40 year old, that is about 180 beats per minute.

Common HIIT mistakes

  • Too little rest for the goal. Shorter rest is not always better. If every round is weaker than the last, the intervals are no longer high intensity.
  • Too many rounds. Stop when your form goes. The last round should look like the first.
  • Skipping the warm up. Five minutes of easy movement first lowers the risk of a strain.
  • Watching the clock. Use a timer with sound cues and full screen color, so your eyes stay on your form.
