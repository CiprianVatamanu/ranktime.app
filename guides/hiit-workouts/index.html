HIIT Workouts: Tabata, EMOM, AMRAP and Ladders

5 guides, each with a free PDF

High intensity interval training mixes hard efforts with short rests. Every format here is a different way of setting the clock: you choose the work and the rest, the minute decides, or the clock is fixed and you do as much as you can. Same exercises, very different workouts. Here is how each one works and how to run it.

The formats at a glance

HIIT intervalsYou set the work, the rest and the rounds. 40 seconds on and 20 off for 10 rounds is 10 minutes
Tabata20 seconds all out, 10 seconds of rest, 8 rounds: 4 minutes, from Izumi Tabata's 1996 study
EMOMA set at the top of every minute, and you rest with what is left. 10 push-ups every minute for 10 minutes is 100
AMRAPAs many rounds as possible before a fixed clock runs out, usually 5 to 20 minutes. Your score is rounds plus extra reps
LadderEvery set a little bigger than the last, and often back down: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1

Where to start

  • Pick your numbers with HIIT intervals: which work to rest ratio fits your goal, and how many rounds to do.
  • Only have 4 minutes? Tabata, as hard as you can make it.
  • Want a score to beat? An AMRAP or an EMOM, done again a few weeks later.
