The Murph Workout: Full Guide, Scaling and Pacing

Updated October 7, 2026 · 7 minute read

Murph is one of the hardest bodyweight workouts there is: a mile run, 600 reps, and another mile run. This guide covers where it comes from, how to split the reps so you can finish, how to scale it, and how to pace it.

What is the Murph workout?

Murph is done in this order, for time:

11 mile run (1.6 km)
2100 pull-ups
3200 push-ups
4300 air squats
51 mile run
OptionalWear a weight vest the whole time, traditionally 20 lb (9 kg)

The two runs are done straight through. The 600 reps in the middle can be split up any way you like, as long as all the pull-ups, push-ups and squats are done before the second run starts.

Where Murph comes from

The workout is named after Navy SEAL Lieutenant Michael P. Murphy, who was killed in Afghanistan on June 28, 2005, and was later awarded the Medal of Honor. He called this workout "Body Armor" and did it wearing his vest. It became a CrossFit Hero workout in his memory, and many gyms around the world now do it together every Memorial Day.

How to split the 600 reps

Almost nobody does 100 pull-ups in a row. Splitting the middle into small rounds keeps every muscle group fresh and makes the workout far easier to pace. The most popular splits:

  • 20 rounds of 5 pull-ups, 10 push-ups, 15 squats. The classic split. Each round takes about a minute or two.
  • 50 rounds of 2 pull-ups, 4 push-ups, 6 squats. Smaller sets, more rounds. Good when push-ups are your weak point, because you never get deep into fatigue.
  • Straight through. All 100 pull-ups, then 200 push-ups, then 300 squats. The hardest way to do it, and the way it was first written.

Whatever split you pick, keep the same ratio: 1 pull-up to 2 push-ups to 3 squats.

How to scale Murph

Murph is a long workout, and finishing it with good form beats racing it with bad form. If the full version is too much today, scale it:

  • Half Murph. Half a mile, 50 pull-ups, 100 push-ups, 150 squats, half a mile.
  • No vest. Do the vest only after you can finish unweighted comfortably.
  • Easier pull-ups. Band assisted pull-ups, jumping pull-ups, or ring rows.
  • Easier push-ups. Hands on a bench or box, or from your knees.
  • Swap the run. Row 2,000 m or bike instead if running hurts.

Pacing tips

  1. Go easy on the first mile. It is only a warm up. Arriving at the pull-up bar out of breath costs you minutes later.
  2. Pick small sets before you need them. Stopping before failure, every time, is what keeps you moving.
  3. Push-ups are where most people stall. If yours start falling apart, make the sets smaller rather than resting longer.
  4. Short, planned rests. A fixed 10 seconds between rounds keeps you honest. Long, random breaks add up fast.
  5. Drink and eat beforehand. Most people take between 45 and 75 minutes. Beginners can take longer, and that is fine.
